# Fertilizing a bonsai

Feeding regularly during the growing season is crucial for a bonsai to survive and thrive. A tree in the ground extends its roots in search of nutrients, but a bonsai is confined to a small pot, so it depends on fertilizer to replace what it takes from the soil.

## What is in a fertilizer

Every fertilizer is built on three elements: nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium, written NPK. Nitrogen drives the growth of leaves and stems, phosphorus supports root growth and the growth of flowers and fruit, and potassium supports the overall health of the plant. Fertilizers can also carry micronutrients such as iron, manganese, boron, molybdenum, zinc and copper.

## When to fertilize

Most bonsai should be fed through the whole growing season, from early spring to mid-autumn. Older, mature trees are fed less often, depending on the species, the season, the stage of development and the health of the tree. Indoor trees, which are usually subtropical and do not experience seasons, grow all year round and can be fed all year round. Do not fertilize a tree that is sick or stressed; wait until it has recovered. Feed less in the cold months.

## Which fertilizer to choose

For outdoor trees, one traditional approach is a relatively high-nitrogen fertilizer such as NPK 10:6:6 in spring, a balanced one such as 6:6:6 in summer, and a low-nitrogen one such as 3:6:6 in autumn. More and more experts prefer a balanced fertilizer throughout the growing season, slowly reducing the amount towards its end.

Match the fertilizer to the tree's stage. A tree in early development needs strong growth, so a relatively strong fertilizer; a tree in late development needs balanced growth for fine ramification, so a balanced fertilizer. A high-phosphorus fertilizer such as 6:10:6 helps promote flowering. A slightly lower nitrogen content, or simply less fertilizer, suits older and more mature trees.

For indoor bonsai, a balanced liquid fertilizer applied as the packaging says works well. Expensive fertilizers tailored for bonsai are not necessary. Any standard houseplant or garden fertilizer will keep a bonsai happy, ideally an organic or seaweed-based one, as long as the NPK ratio is close to balanced, around 5:5:5. Liquid, solid, synthetic and organic fertilizers all work; what matters is following the application guidelines on the product. Biogold is a Japanese organic fertilizer often used for bonsai, with an NPK of 6:7:4, applied as pellets on the soil surface under fertilizer covers.

## How much to apply

The quantity and the frequency depend on the product, so follow its instructions. Apply smaller quantities than ordinary pot plants would get. The tree must be planted in a proper, well-draining bonsai soil, or salts build up. For a tree that is no longer in training, slightly reduce the recommended quantity to balance its growth rather than stimulate it. When using solid fertilizer, fertilizer covers keep it in place. Never overfeed; it has serious consequences for the tree's health.
