# What is a bonsai

Translated literally from Japanese, the word bonsai means "tree that grows on a tray". But a tree does not grow in a pot by itself. The care it receives, watering, frequent fertilizing, repotting and pruning among other things, is what allows a bonsai to express the greatness of trees that live in the wild, in miniature. Bonsai is an art that has been studied and refined for centuries, but anyone who admires nature and enjoys shaping a tree can practise its basic care without a mystical green thumb.

## The three parts of the practice

Growing bonsai has three parts. Cultivation is getting a tree: buying one, collecting one, or raising one from nursery stock, seed or cuttings. Styling is shaping it, mostly by pruning and wiring, to look like a mature tree in miniature. Care is keeping it alive and healthy: placement, watering, fertilizing and repotting. Every species has its own care guidelines, so identifying the exact species of a tree is the first step to caring for it well.

## Indoor and outdoor bonsai

Outdoor bonsai are the majority. They need the cycle of the seasons, a bright spot, and some protection in winter. Indoor bonsai are subtropical or tropical species such as ficus, carmona and Chinese elm, and they need a great deal of light and fairly high humidity. The species decides which kind a tree is, and a tree kept in the wrong place does not survive.

## Where to find help

A local bonsai club is the best place to learn, especially for repotting and styling, which are easier to watch than to read about. Beginners' courses, books and online forums cover the fundamentals; if a tree is doing badly, the first question to answer is which species it is.
