# Watering a bonsai

Watering is the most important part of caring for a bonsai. How often a tree needs water depends on the species, the size of the tree, the size of the pot, the time of year, the soil mixture and the climate, so there is no fixed schedule. Watch each tree individually and water it when it needs it. The habit to avoid is watering every tree every day on a routine, at least until you know exactly what you are doing.

## When to water

Water when the soil gets slightly dry. Do not water while the soil is still wet, but never let the tree dry out completely either. As a beginner, push a finger about one centimetre into the soil to check the moisture; if it feels slightly dry, water. With experience you will see, rather than feel, when a tree needs water. A toothpick or chopstick pushed into the soil works the same way: if the tip comes out damp, wait.

The time of day does not matter. Some growers advise against cold water on soil that is warm from the sun, because it cools the tree, but the rule that counts is to water as soon as the soil is slightly dry, whatever the hour. Water more often in the warm months and less often in the cold months.

## How to water

Once the soil is slightly dry, soak the whole root system thoroughly. Keep watering until water runs out of the drainage holes at the bottom of the pot, and repeat a few minutes later if needed. Pour from above with a watering can with a fine nozzle so the soil is not washed away. An indoor tree can be carried to the kitchen sink, watered thoroughly and put back. The whole root mass must be wetted every time; the excess must drain out of the bottom of the pot.

If you only have a few trees, dip watering is simple and reliable. When the soil begins to look and feel dry, plunge the whole pot under water in a bowl. Once it is thoroughly soaked, lift it out and set it aside, slightly tilted, so the excess drains away. Repeat when the tree starts to dry out again.

Rainwater is the best water because it contains no added chemicals. Ordinary tap water is fine when rainwater is not available; letting tap water stand for twenty-four hours before use lets the chlorine escape. Automated watering systems exist but are expensive.

## Why the soil matters

The soil mixture largely decides how often a tree needs water. Most bonsai thrive in a mix of akadama, pumice and lava rock in the proportions one half, one quarter, one quarter. If you cannot water regularly, use a mix that holds more water, with more akadama or even some compost.

When most of the water runs straight out of the pot, the roots are too compacted for the soil to absorb anything. That tree is pot-bound and needs repotting; more water will not help it.

## In short

Never let a bonsai dry out completely. Wait for the soil to become slightly dry, then water very thoroughly so the entire root mass is reached. Overwatering causes root rot, so do not water soil that is still wet.
