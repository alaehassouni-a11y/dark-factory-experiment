# Where to place a bonsai

Choosing the right spot for a bonsai depends on the local climate and the time of year, but the decisive factor is the tree species, and above all whether it is an indoor or an outdoor tree. Placing an outdoor tree inside, or an indoor tree outside, is a sure way to kill it, so decide where the tree will live before buying or growing it. Knowing the exact species is what lets you look up its care.

## Indoor bonsai

In most houses the only place where an indoor bonsai does well is right at a south-facing window, because plenty of light is crucial to its health. Even a few feet away from the window the light drops sharply, growth slows and the tree eventually dies.

Indoor bonsai are usually subtropical or tropical species such as ficus, carmona or Chinese elm. They also need relatively high humidity, and standing the pot on a humidity tray helps. Keep an indoor tree somewhere the temperature stays constant through the day, away from draughts.

## Outdoor bonsai

Depending on where you live, most trees need to stay outside all year round. The yearly cycle of the seasons is essential to the health of most species, and overprotecting a tree in winter weakens it. As a rule of thumb, put an outdoor bonsai in a bright spot with lots of light. If summers are very hot, afternoon shade helps. In winter the tree may need protection from frost, because small, shallow pots are more exposed to cold than the ground is; even species native to the area may need some shelter in a hard winter.

A tree native to your region is the safest choice for an outdoor bonsai. Bonsai is mostly an outdoor activity, and even in cold countries growers keep outdoor trees outside with winter protection; growing outdoor species indoors is much harder than protecting them outside.

## Light and temperature

Give each tree the light its species needs: some want direct sun, others prefer filtered light or partial shade. Avoid draughts and extremes of temperature. Most bonsai prefer moderate temperatures, roughly between fifteen and twenty-five degrees Celsius, and some species must be sheltered or covered in winter.
