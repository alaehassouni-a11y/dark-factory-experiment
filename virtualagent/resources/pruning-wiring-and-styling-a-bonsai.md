# Pruning, wiring and styling a bonsai

Shaping a tree is the creative part of bonsai and also the most challenging. Mastering pruning and wiring takes years, but the basics can be learned quickly. Bonsai is not a race and not a destination; it is a journey that never ends, and a tree is only ever getting better or getting worse. Your job is to keep it getting better.

## Pruning

Pruning is the single most important technique in bonsai. It shapes the tree and keeps it miniature, so that it looks like a full-size tree in its natural setting. The best time to prune most trees is spring, with some differences between species. For thick branches use concave cutters: they leave a hollow wound that heals much better than a flat cut.

Every cut should be a small improvement to the structure and health of the tree; a bonsai gets better through hundreds of tiny choices made over years. Remove one of two branches growing at the same height on the trunk, a branch with unnatural twists and turns, and any disproportionately thick branch at the top of the tree.

A tree does not have to look "bonsai shaped" at every moment. There is no need to pinch and shorten every branch as soon as it grows. Letting a tree grow freely through spring and early summer, then shortening or removing the long spring growth in a June prune, improves root production and the health of the tree.

## Wiring

Wiring bends and shapes branches. Wrap anodised aluminium or annealed copper wire carefully around a branch and bend it, within limits, to the shape you want. Wiring can be done all year round, but the wire must be removed promptly, before it starts cutting into a branch as the branch thickens.

## Styling a young plant

Almost every bonsai has a clear trunk line, usually one trunk, sometimes a dominant trunk with a secondary one; more than two is rare outside group and forest styles. A shrubby nursery plant has many trunks and branches, and the first task is to find the most pleasing trunk line in it, usually a curved one with flowing movement. Reveal it by removing the competing trunks and branches, keeping in mind which branches you want: a simple convention is that branches sit on the outside of the curves.

Then pinch back new growth to force growth closer to the trunk and make the tree denser and more compact. Foliage on a bonsai is carried in distinct pads, separated from each other. Remove anything growing up out of a pad and anything growing below a branch and its secondary branches, which usually lie in a flat plane. Prune the whole tree so its foliage forms a scalene triangle, with unequal sides and its three corners at different heights.

Go slowly. Never remove more than about a third of the foliage in one session, and leave a few months between sessions; a tree needs its leaves to make energy, and cutting too much weakens it. Shape a tree progressively, in small steps.

## Tools and styles

Use scissors made for bonsai to cut branches and leaves; ordinary scissors damage the tree. Prune in spring and summer, when growth is fastest. There are several classical styles, formal and informal among them, each with its own appearance and shaping techniques.
